## Campagne 1 — PPWR, deadline 12 August 2026

**À envoyer en priorité.** L'obligation générale de recyclabilité de l'article
6(1) s'applique au 12 août 2026. C'est le sujet qui obsède les imprimeurs
packaging en ce moment, et l'Academy répond exactement à la question.

**Objet :** PPWR applies on 12 August. Most printers still read it wrong.

**Preview :** The recyclability duty is not a 2030 topic.

Hi {{first_name}},

There is a widespread misreading of the PPWR going around, and it is worth
sixty seconds of your time.

Most people in our industry believe recyclability becomes an obligation in 2030.
It does not. **Article 6(1) applies from 12 August 2026**: from that date, all
packaging placed on the Union market must be recyclable.

What arrives later is the graded performance layer on top of that duty:

▪ At least grade C from 1 January 2030 (or 24 months after the relevant
delegated acts enter into force, whichever is later)
▪ At least grade B from 1 January 2038

Two more points that catch people out. The grades are **A, B and C only**:
anything below C is described as technically non-recyclable and carries no
letter. And **EN 18120**, although published, is not cited in the Official
Journal, so conforming to it gives you no presumption of conformity today.

We put the whole thing into eight free courses, in eight languages, written
against the official text rather than around it.

## Campagne 3 — Le respect du métier

**Le ton qui a le mieux marché sur LinkedIn.** Pédagogique, pas commercial. À
envoyer en dernier, il réchauffe une liste froide.

**Objet :** Everything a press operator tracks at once

**Preview :** Most people outside the pressroom have no idea.

Hi {{first_name}},

While an offset press runs, the operator is holding a dozen variables in their
head at the same time, and most of them move on their own:

▪ Ink and water balance, shifting with every speed change
▪ Fountain solution: pH, conductivity, temperature
▪ Density and dot gain across every ink zone
▪ Registration, front to back, sheet after sheet
▪ Paper behaviour: humidity, static, stack, feeding
▪ Blankets, plates and rollers, wear you only see in the print
▪ Pressroom temperature and humidity, changing through the shift
▪ Spot colors that leave no room for error
▪ Color drift over a long run, slow and invisible until it is not

And the next job is already waiting, with every minute of makeready measured.

That is a lot to hold, at speed, for eight hours. After 25 years in pressrooms we
still find it impressive.

Closed-loop control does not replace that expertise. It takes the part a machine
does better, measuring and correcting color continuously, so the operator can
put their attention where judgment actually matters.

We are genuinely curious what the hardest part is on your presses. Reply to this
email, we read every answer.
